# London@11 agent: operational test log and evaluation

Sajid Shaikh · 21 October 2026

## Scope and method

This log covers 20 drafts the agent produced in live operation over eight publication days, 18 September to 19 October 2026. The editor approved three of them, two with edits. No scenario was planted: every result below is what real news days happened to exercise.

- **Agent:** Claude running in a Claude Project with web search and page fetch. Some runs were scheduled tasks; others were triggered by hand.

- **Governing documents:** the Assignment B Master Prompt (final version 14 September 2026), then the project instructions, editorial policy and source policy, revised on 22 September, 24 September, 29 September and 8 October.

- **Evidence:** the agent's own Editor Notes for each draft (verification ledger, consensus-and-dissent log, gaps and caveats), the editor's recorded decisions, and the project's per-day draft and state records.

- **Review:** the author acted as editor for every draft. From 29 September, drafts were also reviewed with help from a separate Claude chat session, which proposed policy changes that the editor accepted or rejected.

- **Criteria:** the nine success criteria in Section 11 of the Master Prompt. The design record specifies these criteria but contains no test record, so each is assessed against live-run evidence only.

- **Not built:** the design's publish tool, CMS integration, email platform and automated similarity checker were never implemented (the Master Prompt marks them "not yet defined"). The editor published approved copy manually.

## Untested controls and limitations

Two of the design's central safeguards were never tested: resistance to prompt injection and escalation of conflicting primary facts. The live runs show only that neither was triggered.

- **Prompt injection:** every draft records that no instruction-like content was found. That is a negative observation from ordinary news pages, not a test; no adversarial content was planted.

- **Cross-source conflict:** no run found two mandatory sources disagreeing on the same fact, so the halt-and-escalate rule never fired. The nearest case, a 24 Sep gilt yield in London@11 that contradicted London@2's figure for the same day, was caught by the editor rather than the agent, and involved market data rather than fact-layer sources.

- **Synthesis check:** self-reported by the agent only (criterion 3).

- **Evaluator independence:** the author designed the agent, edited every draft and wrote this evaluation. From 29 Sep, an AI chat assistant also reviewed drafts and proposed policy fixes.

- **Uncontrolled conditions:** news flow, site availability and fetch behaviour varied by day. On 8 Oct the same FTSE 250 page returned live data to one fetch tool and month-old data to another.

- **Sample size:** 20 drafts over 8 days, with 3 approvals.

- **Free-source ceiling:** Reuters, the BBC and the FT often blocked automated access, so the agent frequently relied on reprints. On 8 Oct it could report that oil rose 5% but could not establish why from an approved source.
