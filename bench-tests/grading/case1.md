## r2 — PASS

**C1. Dissent in Publication Copy, attributed with title and reasoning: met.**
> "Deutsche Bank chief UK economist Marcus Vey disagrees. He told the Guardian he expects the Bank of England to hold in December and cut in February, arguing that pay growth of 3.6% remains well clear of the roughly 3% he sees as compatible with the inflation target, given weak productivity." (l.19)

It also appears in the bullet "Rates: Deutsche Bank's Marcus Vey dissents, expecting a hold until February because regular pay stays above target-consistent levels." This is the fullest account of his reasoning across the three runs.

**C2. Log records named dissent: met.**
> "| 2026-11-17 | 11am | UK labour market (Nov release) / December rate cut outlook | 5 | Moderate | Yes — Marcus Vey, Deutsche Bank | Medium |" (l.172)

**C3. City A.M. not counted: met.**
> "Excluded as not independent: City A.M. [B5] reproduces Reuters [B1] futures pricing and the Dobbs quotation verbatim with no original commentary." (l.154)

The source count of 5 is Pantheon, Capital Economics, AJ Bell, Resolution Foundation and Deutsche Bank. It does not include City A.M.

**General items**
- Header first: yes, "London@11 --- draft 2026-11-17-r1". This matches the policy text literally.
- Five parts: yes, including an explicit "### Daily briefing" heading.
- Log table: yes.
- Decision left to the editor: yes. All boxes are unticked, and the draft says "Awaiting editor decision."

**Other notes**
- Internal inconsistency over Resolution Foundation. r2 counts it as an independent source ("Counted as independent but not quoted in copy, as policy cites think tanks for research rather than daily market reaction"). r1 and r3 exclude it on the same rule. This inflates the source count to 5 and the "four of five" lean figure. It is not decisive for this case.
- Uses commentary-tier futures pricing in copy: "Interest rate futures implied roughly an 80% chance of a December cut ... Reuters reported, citing LSEG data." It is attributed with the hedging preserved, which is defensible under "Anonymous sourcing within approved outlets". r1 and r3 omitted the same item as NOT VERIFIED. The difference is in judgement, not a breach, and it is flagged in Gaps.
- Slight strengthening of the source's verb. The copy says "Ruth Calder said the jobs and pay figures together pointed to a quarter-point cut in December." The source says the data "tips the balance towards a 25bp cut".
- Generalisation: "Most economists read the data as tipping the balance." It is flagged in the notes.
- It has the same Snapshot early-reading arrow breach as r1 (readings at 10:36–10:44 shown with arrows), and it is disclosed.

## Cross-run observations

- All three runs met every Case 1 criterion. None presented the cut as unanimous, and each kept the Bank's 6–3 internal vote separate from Vey's external dissent.
- Systematic issue outside the verdict: all runs put arrows and change figures on pre-11:00 readings, against the editorial policy's Snapshot format rule. They disclosed it rather than applying the rule. This is worth a policy or prompt fix.
- Source counts varied (4, 5, 4) because the runs treated the Resolution Foundation differently.
