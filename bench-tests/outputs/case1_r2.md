## UK jobless rate hits 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Weaker hiring and slower wages strengthen the case for a December cut by the Bank of England, though one leading economist urges caution.**

### Daily briefing

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, up from 5.0% in the previous quarter, official figures showed. That is already above the 5.1% average that independent forecasters expect for the final three months of the year.

Annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%. The early estimate of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, and vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000 in the three months to October.

The figures go to the heart of the Bank of England's divide. It held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%.

Most economists read the data as tipping the balance. Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs told Reuters it was "the clearest signal yet that the labour market has turned", adding that slowing pay removed the main obstacle cited by the hawks. He now expects a cut to 3.5% next month. Capital Economics UK economist Ruth Calder said the jobs and pay figures together pointed to a quarter-point cut in December, while AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson said the door to a December move "looks to have swung open".

Deutsche Bank chief UK economist Marcus Vey disagrees. He told the Guardian he expects the Bank of England to hold in December and cut in February, arguing that pay growth of 3.6% remains well clear of the roughly 3% he sees as compatible with the inflation target, given weak productivity. "One soft quarter of labour data is not enough for a majority to move before Christmas," he said.

Markets lean towards the majority view. Interest rate futures implied roughly an 80% chance of a December cut, up from around 55% before the release, Reuters reported, citing LSEG data. The two-year gilt yield, the maturity most sensitive to Bank Rate expectations, was 6 basis points lower at 3.62%, and sterling was down 0.35% against the dollar.

Wednesday's October inflation figures are the next test of whether price pressures back the majority or Mr Vey's caution.

### What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% and slowing pay make a December cut the likelier outcome for most economists.
- **Rates:** Deutsche Bank's Marcus Vey dissents, expecting a hold until February because regular pay stays above target-consistent levels.
- **Bonds:** The two-year gilt yield, most sensitive to rate expectations, is down 6bp at 3.62%.
- **Inflation:** Wednesday's October price figures will show whether the Bank of England has reason to wait.
