## UK year-to-date borrowing runs £4.6bn below last year's pace

**October's £8.2bn debt interest bill weighs on the public finances, while shoppers bought slightly more and long-dated gilt yields climbed.**

Government borrowing in the financial year to October totalled £121.4bn, £4.6bn less than in the same period last year, official figures show. Public sector net debt was provisionally estimated at 95.8% of GDP at the end of the month, and central government debt interest payable reached £8.2bn in October alone.

That interest bill is where the pressure sits. Institute for Fiscal Studies research economist Isaac Delaney said the year-to-date figure remains below last year's, "but the margin is narrowing".

Bond markets sharpened the point on Friday morning. The 30-year gilt yield rose 5 basis points to 5.18% and the 10-year climbed 4bp to 4.46%, while the two-year edged up 1bp to 3.60%. Higher long-dated yields, if sustained, raise what the government pays on new long-term debt. Investors are still buying it: Thursday's £4.25bn sale of a 2035 gilt drew bids worth 2.81 times the amount on offer.

Consumers offered only a slight lift. Retail sales volumes rose 0.2% in October, after a 0.6% fall in September that was revised from an initial 0.5% drop. Excluding fuel, volumes rose 0.1%. AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson called it "a modest bounce after September's fall".

For the Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% this month as three of nine policymakers voted for a cut, a barely growing consumer and climbing market interest rates pull in opposite directions before its next decision on 17 December.

Read together, the figures show a fiscal cushion that exists but is exposed: borrowing is still below last year's, yet a heavy interest bill and rising long-term yields put it at risk, while households are adding little to growth. The FTSE 100 was 0.18% lower at 10,388 by mid-morning.
