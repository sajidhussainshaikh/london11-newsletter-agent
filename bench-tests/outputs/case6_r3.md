## UK unemployment rises to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**The number of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, strengthening the case for a Bank of England rate cut next month.**

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, from 5.0% in the previous quarter, the Office for National Statistics said. That is already above the 5.1% that independent forecasters, on average, expected for the final quarter of the year, according to the Treasury's November comparison of forecasts.

The weakness runs through the release. An early estimate showed the number of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October. Vacancies dropped by 18,000 to 701,000 in the three months to October. Annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

Softer hiring and slower pay growth go to the heart of the Bank of England's debate. The Bank held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%. Interest rate futures moved to price roughly an 80% chance of a December cut, up from around 55% before the release, Reuters reported, citing LSEG data.

Economists read the figures the same way. Capital Economics UK economist Ruth Calder told Reuters that "firms are now shedding staff, not just freezing hiring", and that she expected a quarter-point cut in December. Resolution Foundation senior economist Hannah Pryce told the Guardian that slower pay growth "takes away the Bank's last real reason to wait". Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs said the data strengthened Pantheon's call for a cut to 3.5%, while AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson called the vacancy fall "the clearest sign yet that employers are retrenching".

In markets, two-year gilt yields, the most sensitive to Bank Rate expectations, were 6bp lower at 3.62%, and sterling was down 0.35% against the dollar. The FTSE 100 was 0.32% higher.

The next test comes on Wednesday, when October inflation figures are due. A cooler reading would leave little standing between the Bank and a December cut; a hotter one would strengthen the hand of the six policymakers who voted to hold this month.
