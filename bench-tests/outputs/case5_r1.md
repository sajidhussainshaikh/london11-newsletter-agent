## Debt interest bill hits £8.2bn, long gilt yields climb

**Year-to-date borrowing runs £4.6 billion below last year's pace, while retail sales volumes rose 0.2% last month after September's fall.**

Government borrowing in the first seven months of the financial year totalled £121.4 billion, £4.6 billion less than in the same period last year, the Office for National Statistics (ONS) said on Friday. Public sector net debt was provisionally estimated at 95.8% of GDP at the end of October.

Carrying that debt is expensive. Central government debt interest payable was £8.2 billion in October alone. Gilt markets offered no respite on Friday morning. The 10-year yield rose 4 basis points to 4.46% and the 30-year yield 5 basis points to 5.18%, while the two-year yield edged up just 1 basis point to 3.60%. Higher yields mean lower gilt prices and dearer new long-term borrowing for the government. Thursday's sale of £4.25 billion of a 2035 gilt was covered 2.81 times, the Debt Management Office said.

Shoppers provided only slight relief. Retail sales volumes in Great Britain rose 0.2% in October, the ONS said, after a 0.6% fall in September, revised from an initially reported 0.5% drop. Excluding fuel, volumes rose 0.1%. AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson called it "a modest bounce after September's fall".

Taken together, the figures describe households spending cautiously while the state's debt costs are heavy and long-term market borrowing costs rose on Friday. That squeezes from both sides: subdued spending offers little lift to growth, while higher yields add to the cost of new debt.

It also sharpens the choice for the Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%. Soft spending hands those three a fresh argument before the next decision on 17 December.

## What matters by 11

- **Public finances:** Year-to-date borrowing is £4.6bn below last year, but October's £8.2bn debt interest bill underlines the cost of high debt.
- **Bonds:** Long gilt yields rose up to 5bp as the two-year barely moved, raising the cost of new long-term government borrowing.
- **Shoppers:** October's 0.2% rise in retail volumes only partly reverses September's revised 0.6% fall, signalling cautious households.
- **Rates:** Soft spending and rising long yields frame the Bank of England's 17 December decision after November's 6-3 hold.
