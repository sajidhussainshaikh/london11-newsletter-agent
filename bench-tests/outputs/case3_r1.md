## UK jobless rate hits 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Weaker hiring and slower wage rises bolster the case for a December cut by the Bank of England, before Wednesday's inflation figures.**

### Daily briefing

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September from 5.0% in the previous quarter, already above the 5.1% that independent forecasters, on average, expect for the final quarter of the year.

The softening runs right across the Office for National Statistics figures. The early estimate of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000, and annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

That lands squarely on a divided Bank of England. It held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November, with six policymakers voting to hold and three backing a cut to 3.5%. Rising joblessness alongside cooling pay is the combination most likely to make some of that majority reconsider.

Economists read the data the same way. "This is the clearest signal yet that the labour market has turned," Kieran Dobbs, chief UK economist at Pantheon Macroeconomics, told Reuters, adding that he now expects a cut to 3.5% next month. Capital Economics UK economist Ruth Calder said the figures "tip the balance towards a 25bp cut in December", while AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson said the door to a December cut "looks to have swung open".

Markets are leaning the same way. Two-year gilt yields, the most sensitive to rate expectations, were down 6bp at 3.62% and 10-year yields 5bp lower at 4.41%, while sterling slipped 0.35% to $1.3120. Lower gilt yields, if sustained, tend to pull down the swap rates off which fixed-rate mortgages are priced.

The next test comes on Wednesday, when October inflation figures will show whether softer pay is already feeding through to prices, a month before the Bank of England's 17 December decision.

### What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% and a 42,000 payroll drop point to a labour market loosening faster than forecast.
- **Rates:** Pantheon and Capital Economics now expect a December cut, putting the six who voted to hold under pressure.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields led the curve lower, the clearest market sign of firmer December cut bets.
- **Inflation:** Wednesday's October figures will show whether cooling pay is already easing price pressure before December's decision.
