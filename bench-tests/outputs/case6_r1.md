## UK jobless rate climbs to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October while vacancies shrank, sharpening the case for a Bank of England cut next month.**

### Daily briefing

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, up from 5.0% in the previous quarter, with every main gauge of the jobs market now pointing the same way.

Annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%. The Office for National Statistics' early estimate showed payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, while vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000.

The jobless rate is already above the 5.1% that independent forecasters, on average, expect for the final quarter of the year, according to HM Treasury's monthly comparison of forecasts published last week.

That shifts the arithmetic for the Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November in a 6-3 vote, with three policymakers backing a cut to 3.5%. Resolution Foundation senior economist Hannah Pryce told the Guardian the slowdown in pay growth "takes away the Bank's last real reason to wait". Capital Economics UK economist Ruth Calder told Reuters the payrolls fall showed "firms are now shedding staff, not just freezing hiring", and said she expected a quarter-point cut in December.

Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs said the data strengthened the firm's long-standing call for a cut to 3.5% in December, while AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson said a December move looked increasingly likely.

Gilts rallied. The two-year yield, the maturity most sensitive to expectations for Bank Rate, fell 6bp to 3.62%, outpacing a 5bp drop in the 10-year yield to 4.41%. Sterling slipped 0.35% to $1.3120, and the FTSE 100 rose 0.32% to 10,412.

Wednesday's October inflation figures now carry much of the weight. With pay growth cooling and employers trimming payrolls, a stubborn price reading is the clearest remaining argument for holding rates when the Bank of England next decides on 17 December.

### What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment rose to 5.2% and October payrolls shrank by 42,000, a labour market loosening on every main measure.
- **Rates:** Slower pay growth of 3.6% clears a path to a December cut, which economists and analysts now broadly expect.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields fell 6bp to 3.62%, outpacing longer maturities, the pattern expected when traders price nearer rate cuts.
- **Inflation:** Wednesday's October CPI figures are now the main remaining test before the Bank of England's 17 December decision.
