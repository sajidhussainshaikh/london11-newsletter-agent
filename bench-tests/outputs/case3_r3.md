## UK jobless rate climbs to 5.2% as pay growth cools

Rising unemployment and slowing wages strengthen the case for a Bank of England cut in December, with inflation figures due on Wednesday.

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, up from 5.0% in the previous quarter, according to the Office for National Statistics. That is already above the 5.1% average that independent forecasters surveyed by HM Treasury this month expect for the final quarter of the year.

The weakness ran through the release. Early estimates show the number of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October compared with September's revised figure, while vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000 in the three months to October. Pay is cooling too: annual growth in regular earnings, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

The figures sharpen the debate before the Bank of England's next decision on 17 December, after it held Bank Rate at 3.75% this month by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%. Economists read the data as tilting the balance towards a move. Kieran Dobbs, chief UK economist at Pantheon Macroeconomics, told Reuters it was "the clearest signal yet that the labour market has turned" and that he now expects a cut to 3.5% next month. Ruth Calder, UK economist at Capital Economics, said the labour market was loosening faster than the Bank of England had projected, adding that the data "tips the balance towards a 25bp cut in December". AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson said the door to a December cut "looks to have swung open".

Gilts were firmer by mid-morning. The two-year yield, the most sensitive to rate expectations, was down 6bp at 3.62%, and the 10-year down 5bp at 4.41%. Sterling was 0.35% weaker against the dollar at $1.3120.

The next test is Wednesday's October inflation release at 07:00. A soft reading would strengthen the hand of the three policymakers who backed a cut; a firm one would give the six who voted to hold grounds to wait.

### What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% has already overshot forecasters' year-end average, pointing to a labour market loosening faster than expected.
- **Rates:** Pantheon, Capital Economics and AJ Bell all see the case for a December Bank of England cut strengthening.
- **Pay:** Regular pay growth slowing to 3.6% from 3.9% eases a domestic inflation pressure ahead of Wednesday's figures.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields, the most rate-sensitive maturity, led the curve lower, down 6bp at 3.62%.
