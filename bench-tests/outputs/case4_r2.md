## Jobless rate rises to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Falling vacancies and shrinking payrolls strengthen the case for a Bank of England cut in December, with inflation figures due on Wednesday.**

UK unemployment rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, up from 5.0% in the previous quarter, the Office for National Statistics said. That is already above the 5.1% average that independent forecasters expected for the final quarter of the year, according to the Treasury's November comparison of forecasts.

The early estimate of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October from September, and vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000 in the three months to October. Annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

Slower pay growth bears directly on the Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November, when three of its nine policymakers voted for a cut to 3.5%. Economists now broadly expect a cut at its next decision on 17 December. Capital Economics UK economist Ruth Calder told Reuters the payrolls fall showed "firms are now shedding staff, not just freezing hiring", and that she expected a quarter-point cut. Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs said slowing private-sector regular pay strengthened his firm's call for a December cut to 3.5%. AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson called a December cut increasingly likely, while Resolution Foundation senior economist Hannah Pryce told the Guardian that weaker hiring of young workers was driving the rise in joblessness.

Interest rate futures priced roughly an 80% chance of a December cut, up from about 55% before the release, Reuters reported, citing LSEG data. Gilt yields were lower across the curve by mid-morning, led by a 6bp fall in the two-year yield to 3.62%, the maturity most sensitive to the outlook for Bank Rate. Sterling was 0.35% weaker against the dollar at $1.3120.

Hewson said a cut would be welcome for borrowers coming off fixed-rate deals; lower gilt yields, if sustained, also tend to feed through swap rates into cheaper fixed mortgages. October's inflation figures, due from the ONS at 07:00 on Wednesday, are the next test of whether the Bank moves in December.

## What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Higher unemployment, fewer payrolled employees and fewer vacancies show a labour market that is now shrinking.
- **Rates:** Pay growth of 3.6% weakens the case for holding, making a December cut the central expectation among economists.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields led falls across the curve, down 6bp at 3.62%, the maturity most sensitive to Bank Rate.
- **Households:** Lower gilt yields, if sustained, should ease fixed mortgage pricing through swap rates for borrowers remortgaging.
