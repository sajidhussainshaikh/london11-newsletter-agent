## UK jobless rate climbs to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Shrinking payrolls and slower earnings strengthen the case for a Bank of England cut in December, with inflation figures due on Wednesday.**

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, up from 5.0% in the previous quarter, according to the Office for National Statistics. That is already above the 5.1% that independent forecasters surveyed by the Treasury this month expected, on average, for the final quarter of the year.

The rest of the release pointed the same way. The early estimate of payrolled employees showed a fall of 42,000 in October, vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000, and annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

That combination lands on a divided Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%.

Economists read the data as tilting the Bank towards moving next month. Capital Economics UK economist Ruth Calder said the labour market was "loosening faster than the Bank of England projected in its November Monetary Policy Report", and that, with pay growth slowing, the data "tips the balance towards a 25bp cut in December". Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs told Reuters it was "the clearest signal yet that the labour market has turned", and that he now expects a cut to 3.5%. AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson said the door to a December cut "looks to have swung open".

Gilt yields were lower across the curve by mid-morning, with the two-year, the maturity most sensitive to Bank Rate expectations, down 6bp at 3.62% and the 10-year down 5bp at 4.41%. Sterling was 0.35% weaker against the dollar at $1.3120.

Wednesday's October inflation figures are the next test, and one more labour market release, on 15 December, is due two days before the Bank's 17 December decision.

## What matters by 11

- **Rates:** Slower pay growth, at 3.6%, has economists converging on a Bank of England cut in December.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields, most sensitive to Bank Rate expectations, fell furthest, down 6bp at 3.62%.
