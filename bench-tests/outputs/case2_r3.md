## UK jobless rate hits 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Weaker hiring and slower wage gains strengthen the case for a Bank of England cut next month, with inflation figures due tomorrow.**

### Daily briefing

The UK unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, from 5.0% in the previous quarter, the Office for National Statistics (ONS) said. That is already above the 5.1% average that independent forecasters, in the Treasury's latest survey, expect for the final quarter of the year.

The weakness ran across the release. The early estimate of payrolled employees showed a fall of 42,000 in October, and vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000 in the three months to October. Annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

The figures land on a divided Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%. Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs said slowing pay growth "removes the main obstacle the hawks had pointed to", calling the data "the clearest signal yet that the labour market has turned". He now expects a cut to 3.5% in December; the Bank's next decision is on 17 December.

Gilt yields fell. The two-year yield, the maturity most sensitive to the rate outlook, was 6 basis points lower at 3.62% by mid-morning, against a 5 basis point fall in the 10-year yield to 4.41%. Gilt yields move closely with the swap rates on which fixed-rate mortgages are priced, so a sustained fall would ease costs for households refinancing.

The next test comes on Wednesday, when the ONS publishes October's consumer price inflation figures. A soft reading would reinforce the signal from the jobs market; a firm one would complicate it. The Debt Management Office then auctions the 4½% Treasury Gilt 2035 on Thursday.

### What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% and falling payrolls show a labour market already weaker than forecasters expected by year-end.
- **Rates:** Slower pay growth, at 3.6%, bolsters the three Bank of England policymakers who backed a cut this month.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields fell 6bp to 3.62%, outpacing longer maturities, the typical pattern when rate expectations shift.
- **Inflation:** Wednesday's October CPI figures will show whether price pressures are easing alongside the jobs market.
