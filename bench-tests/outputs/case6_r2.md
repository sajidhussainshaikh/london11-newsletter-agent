## UK jobless rate hits 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Falling payrolls and fewer vacancies strengthen the case for a Bank of England cut in December, a day before October's inflation figures.**

### Daily briefing

UK unemployment rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, from 5.0% in the previous quarter, the Office for National Statistics said. That already exceeds the 5.1% that independent forecasters, in the Treasury's latest average, expected for the final quarter of the year.

The detail suggests employers are cutting jobs rather than just pausing recruitment. The early estimate of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, while vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000 in the three months to October. Regular pay growth, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

Slowing pay matters most for the Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% this month by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut. Economists now see December's decision as live. Ruth Calder, UK economist at Capital Economics, told Reuters the payrolls fall showed "firms are now shedding staff, not just freezing hiring", and that she expected a quarter-point cut. Kieran Dobbs, chief UK economist at Pantheon Macroeconomics, said the figures strengthened his firm's forecast, unchanged since October, of a cut to 3.5%. Hannah Pryce, senior economist at the Resolution Foundation, told the Guardian that weaker hiring of young workers was driving the rise, and that slower pay growth "takes away the Bank's last real reason to wait".

Gilts rallied. Two-year yields, the most sensitive to interest-rate expectations, were 6bp lower at 3.62% by mid-morning, and 10-year yields 5bp lower at 4.41%. Sterling slipped 0.35% to $1.3120, while the FTSE 100 was 0.3% higher. Lower short-dated yields, if sustained, would feed through swap rates into cheaper fixed-rate mortgages.

Wednesday's October inflation figures are the next test before the Bank of England's 17 December decision: a soft reading would leave few obstacles to a cut.

### What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2%, falling payrolls and fewer vacancies mark a clear turn for the worse in the labour market.
- **Rates:** Pay growth easing to 3.6% removes a key obstacle to a Bank of England cut on 17 December.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields fell 6bp to 3.62%, leading a rally concentrated at the short end of the curve.
