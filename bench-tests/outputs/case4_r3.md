## Jobless rate climbs to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Rising unemployment, falling vacancies and slowing wage gains strengthen the case for the Bank of England cutting borrowing costs next month.**

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, from 5.0% in the previous three months, official figures show. That is already above the 5.1% that independent forecasters, on average, expect for the final quarter of the year, according to the Treasury's latest comparison of forecasts.

Other measures point the same way. The early estimate of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, while vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000. Annual growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

The figures bear directly on the Bank of England's next decision, on 17 December. The Bank held Bank Rate at 3.75% this month by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%, and economists now broadly expect that view to prevail.

Capital Economics UK economist Ruth Calder told Reuters the payrolls fall showed "firms are now shedding staff, not just freezing hiring", and that she expected a quarter-point cut in December. Resolution Foundation senior economist Hannah Pryce told the Guardian the slowdown in pay growth "takes away the Bank's last real reason to wait". Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs said the data strengthened his firm's call for a cut to 3.5%.

Gilts rallied, led by the shorter maturities most sensitive to rate expectations: the two-year yield was down 6bp at 3.62% and the 10-year down 5bp at 4.41%. Sterling was 0.35% weaker against the dollar at $1.3120.

For households, AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson said a cut would be welcome news for borrowers coming off fixed-rate deals. Fixed mortgage rates are priced off swap rates, which move closely with gilt yields.

The next test comes on Wednesday, when October inflation figures are published.

## What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% has already overshot forecasters' end-of-year average, a labour market cooling faster than assumed.
- **Rates:** Economists broadly expect a cut to 3.5% on 17 December after falling payrolls and slowing pay.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields fell most, down 6bp at 3.62%, the maturity most tied to rate expectations.
- **Inflation:** October's consumer price figures on Wednesday are the next test of the case for a December cut.
- **Calendar:** The next jobs figures land on 15 December, two days before the Bank of England's decision.
