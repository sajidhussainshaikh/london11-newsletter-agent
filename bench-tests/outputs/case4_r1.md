## UK unemployment climbs to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Falling payrolls and slower wages leave the Bank of England under mounting pressure for a December cut in borrowing costs.**

### Daily briefing

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, up from 5.0% in the previous three months, according to the Office for National Statistics. That is already above the 5.1% average that independent forecasters, in the Treasury's latest comparison, expect for the final quarter of the year.

The detail suggests employers are cutting, not merely pausing. An early estimate shows the number of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, while vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000. Growth in regular pay, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%.

That shifts the ground under the Bank of England, which held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November with three of its nine policymakers voting for a cut to 3.5%. Cooling pay eases one of the pressures that can keep inflation elevated.

Economists read the figures the same way. Ruth Calder, UK economist at Capital Economics, told Reuters that firms "are now shedding staff, not just freezing hiring", and said she expected a quarter-point cut in December. Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs said the data, particularly slower private-sector regular pay, strengthened the firm's call for a cut to 3.5%. AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson called the vacancy fall "the clearest sign yet that employers are retrenching".

Short-dated gilts, the part of the market most sensitive to rate expectations, led yields lower: the two-year yield was 6 basis points down at 3.62% by mid-morning, with the 10-year 5bp lower at 4.41%. Sterling was 0.35% weaker against the dollar at $1.3120.

A December cut would help borrowers coming off fixed-rate deals, although new fixed mortgages are priced off swap rates, which move closely with gilt yields, rather than off Bank Rate itself.

Wednesday's October inflation figures are the next test. A soft reading would clear the path to a December cut; a firm one would strengthen the hand of the six policymakers who voted to hold.

### What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% and a 42,000 payroll drop show employers shedding staff, not just pausing recruitment.
- **Rates:** With regular pay growth down to 3.6%, economists now treat a December Bank of England cut as the base case.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields fell 6bp to 3.62%, the biggest move on a curve most sensitive to rate expectations.
- **Inflation:** Wednesday's October figures are now the main hurdle between the Bank of England and a December cut.
