## UK unemployment climbs to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Britain's jobless rate already exceeds forecasters' year-end average, while slower wage gains test the Bank of England's case for holding borrowing costs.**

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, up from 5.0% in the previous three-month period, according to the Office for National Statistics. That is already above the 5.1% that independent forecasters, on average, expect for the final quarter of the year, according to the Treasury's latest comparison of forecasts.

Pay is cooling with it. Annual growth in regular earnings, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%. An early estimate showed the number of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, while vacancies dropped by 18,000 over the quarter to 701,000.

The figures arrive a month before the Bank of England's next decision, on 17 December. Earlier this month it held Bank Rate at 3.75% by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%. A sustained slowdown in pay is the kind of evidence that could test that majority.

Pantheon Macroeconomics chief UK economist Kieran Dobbs called the data "the clearest signal yet that the labour market has turned", arguing that slower pay growth removes the main objection of those favouring a hold. He now expects a cut to 3.5% in December. Capital Economics said the labour market was "loosening faster than the Bank of England projected".

Gilt yields were lower by mid-morning, led by shorter maturities, which are the most sensitive to interest-rate expectations: the two-year yield was down 6 basis points at 3.62% and the 10-year down 5bp at 4.41%. Gilt yields move closely with the swap rates lenders use to price fixed-rate mortgages. Sterling was 0.35% weaker against the dollar.

October's inflation figures, due on Wednesday, will show whether price growth is cooling alongside pay before the Bank of England meets.

## What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% already tops forecasters' year-end average, a faster deterioration than they had pencilled in.
- **Rates:** Cooling pay strengthens the hand of the three Bank of England policymakers who voted for a cut this month.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields fell 6bp to 3.62%, the biggest move across the gilt curve this morning.
- **Inflation:** Wednesday's October figures will show whether price pressures are easing alongside the jobs market.
