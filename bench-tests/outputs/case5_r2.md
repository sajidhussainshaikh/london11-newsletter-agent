## UK debt interest bill reaches £8.2bn in October

**Government borrowing so far this financial year is £121.4bn, £4.6bn below last year, while retail sales volumes edged up 0.2%.**

### Daily briefing

Central government debt interest payable reached £8.2bn in October, according to the Office for National Statistics. That single month's bill shows how much of the public purse now goes on servicing past borrowing. Public sector net debt, excluding public sector banks, was provisionally estimated at 95.8% of GDP at the end of the month.

The broader trend is less stark. Borrowing in the financial year to October totalled £121.4bn, £4.6bn less than in the same period last year, so the deficit is running below last year's level seven months into the financial year.

The bond market offered no relief on that bill. By mid-morning the 10-year gilt yield had risen 4 basis points to 4.46% and the 30-year yield 5 basis points to 5.18%, lifting the cost of new long-term government borrowing. Demand at auction held up: Thursday's £4.25bn sale of a 2035 gilt drew bids worth 2.81 times the amount on offer.

Households are giving growth only modest support. Retail sales volumes rose 0.2% in October, after a 0.6% fall in September that was revised from an initial 0.5%. Excluding fuel, volumes rose 0.1%. AJ Bell head of financial analysis Danni Hewson called it "a modest bounce after September's fall".

Taken together, a heavy interest bill and hesitant shoppers set the backdrop for the Bank of England's next decision on 17 December. It held Bank Rate at 3.75% on 5 November, when three of nine policymakers backed a cut to 3.5%. Gilt yields, which drive the cost of new government debt, are set by markets rather than directly by Bank Rate, so a cut would not by itself lower that bill.

The FTSE 100 was down 0.18% at 10,388 by mid-morning.

### What matters by 11

- **Debt:** An £8.2bn monthly interest bill shows how heavily servicing costs now weigh on the public finances.
- **Borrowing:** Year-to-date borrowing of £121.4bn is £4.6bn below last year, a cushion that is still intact.
- **Bonds:** Long-dated gilt yields rose up to 5 basis points, nudging up the cost of new government borrowing.
- **Shoppers:** October's 0.2% rise in retail volumes only partly reverses September's 0.6% fall, pointing to cautious households.
