## UK jobless rate climbs to 5.2% as pay growth cools

**Weaker hiring and slowing earnings sharpen the debate over a December cut in borrowing costs, with Wednesday's inflation figures next.**

Britain's unemployment rate rose to 5.2% in the three months to September, from 5.0% in the previous quarter, official figures show. That already overshoots the 5.1% average of independent forecasts compiled by the Treasury for the final three months of the year.

The weakness ran deeper than the headline. Regular pay growth, excluding bonuses, slowed to 3.6% from 3.9%. An early estimate showed the number of payrolled employees fell by 42,000 in October, while vacancies dropped by 18,000 on the quarter to 701,000 in the three months to October. Taken together, the figures point to a labour market cooling on several fronts at once rather than a one-off jump in joblessness.

The data arrive less than a fortnight after the Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75% by six votes to three, with the minority backing a cut to 3.5%. Pay growth is a key gauge of domestic price pressure, so its slowdown bears directly on that split.

Kieran Dobbs, chief UK economist at Pantheon Macroeconomics, called the data "the clearest signal yet that the labour market has turned" and said he now expects a cut to 3.5% in December.

Gilt yields were lower across the curve in morning trading. The two-year yield, the maturity most sensitive to Bank Rate expectations, fell 6bp to 3.62%, against a 5bp fall in the 10-year yield to 4.41%. Sterling slipped 0.35% against the dollar to $1.3120.

For households, lower gilt yields tend to feed through to the swap rates that price fixed-rate mortgages. The next test is Wednesday's October inflation figures, ahead of the Bank of England's decision on 17 December.

## What matters by 11

- **Jobs:** Unemployment at 5.2% has already overshot the 5.1% that forecasters averaged for the year's final quarter.
- **Pay:** Regular pay growth slowing to 3.6% eases one of the main domestic inflation pressures the Bank of England watches.
- **Rates:** With three policymakers already backing a cut, Wednesday's inflation data carries extra weight for December's decision.
- **Bonds:** Two-year gilt yields fell 6bp to 3.62%, the biggest move across the curve this morning.
